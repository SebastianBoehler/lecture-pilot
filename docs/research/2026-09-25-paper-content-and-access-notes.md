# Paper content and access notes

Prepared 25 September 2026 after the discovery bibliography. These are reading
notes to support later evidence-based product work; they are not a ranking of
methods or a claim that LecturePilot is effective. Each entry states how much
of the source was actually accessible. Full-text notes report the study/task
and outcome; abstract-only entries stay at abstract level.

Access labels:

- **Full text read:** article or manuscript text was available in an open
  repository or publisher page.
- **Abstract/partial only:** an abstract, publisher summary, or indexed excerpt
  was visible, but I did not retrieve and read the complete article.
- **Already read:** fuller methods and limits are in one of the earlier
  linked LecturePilot research notes.

## Learning, retrieval, spacing, and self-regulation

- **Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017), practice-testing meta-analysis —
  full text read.** The synthesis included 118 articles, 272 independent effect
  sizes and 15,427 participants, across laboratory and classroom contexts and
  school levels. Practice testing showed a random-effects estimate of *g* = .70
  overall, with high heterogeneity (I² about 81%). Estimates differed by
  comparator (about *g* = .51 versus restudy/rereading and .93 versus filler/no
  activity); transfer and retention estimates were not significantly different.
  Most effects were from laboratory studies and retention tests. The pooled
  estimate is not a forecast for a particular tutor feature.
  [Open paper copy](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2017-adesope.pdf) ·
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306).
- **Cepeda et al. (2006), distributed-practice meta-analysis — full manuscript
  read.** It synthesizes 317 experiments across 184 articles and 839
  assessments. The relation between practice spacing and final retention
  depends jointly on spacing and the later test delay; optimal spacing grew as
  the retention interval grew. Most observed tests had short delays, so evidence
  for very long retention intervals was sparse. It concerns verbal recall and
  does not prescribe a fixed LecturePilot schedule.
  [Open manuscript](https://www.escholarship.org/content/qt3rr6q10c/qt3rr6q10c.pdf) ·
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354).
- **Panadero (2017), SRL models review — full text read.** A conceptual
  comparison of six models, not a single intervention. The models commonly
  describe preparation/planning, performance with monitoring/control, and
  appraisal/adaptation, while differing in emphasis on motivation, emotion,
  context and metacognition. The author notes substantial overlap among models
  and limited evidence testing context differences; this is a framework source,
  not a feature-level effect estimate.
  [Open article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00422/full).
- **Nückles et al. (2020), writing-to-learn/SRL review — full text read.** This
  synthesizes a research program (16 experimental and four correlational
  studies), not one trial. Structured journal prompts aimed to elicit cognitive
  and metacognitive strategies; the review describes using worked examples,
  metacognitive information and adapting/fading prompts with expertise. In one
  12-week university intervention, differences present in the first six weeks
  diminished in the second six weeks. The authors discuss internalized prompts
  potentially becoming redundant. The underlying studies are heterogeneous.
  [Open article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09541-1).
- **Karpicke & Blunt (2011), retrieval versus concept mapping — partial only.**
  The PubMed abstract reports that retrieval practice outperformed elaborative
  concept mapping on meaningful learning questions, including comprehension and
  inference. The accessible supporting material confirms one experiment used
  80 Purdue undergraduates and a short science text. The complete main article
  was not available through the attempted author link, so the abstract is the
  limit of this note; do not extend its result to whole-course learning.
  [Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/) ·
  [supporting material](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_ScienceSupportingMaterial.pdf).
- **Dunlosky et al. (2013), learning-technique review — publisher summary only.**
  The APS summary says the review compared ten common techniques and rated
  practice testing and distributed practice as high utility. The complete
  article was not read in this pass.
  [Publisher page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/learning-techniques.html).
- **Jansen et al. (2019), higher-education SRL meta-analysis — abstract only.**
  The abstract reports SRL interventions improved SRL activity and achievement,
  with activity only partly mediating achievement; most tested moderators did
  not explain significant variance. Effect sizes, study counts and methods were
  not extracted because the full article was not accessible in this pass.
  [Publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2019.100292).
- **Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill (2003), worked examples and fading — full text
  read from the archived author copy.** Two experiments tested faded worked
  examples and prompts to explain underlying principles. The combination
  improved near and far transfer in the structured probability tasks. The
  authors caution that the prompts fit domains with identifiable principles;
  forcing explanation at every step could become redundant.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774).
- **Toppino, LaVan & Iaconelli (2018), learner choices of retrieval/spacing —
  IU EBSCO abstract only.** EBSCO exposed the peer-reviewed record and abstract:
  choices between restudy and retrieval varied with item difficulty and spacing;
  learners did not always choose the spacing that best supported later recall.
  Full text was not retrieved.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-018-0828-2).
- **Martins van Jaarsveld et al. (2025), scripted agent for goal monitoring —
  full author manuscript read from the Radboud repository.** Eighty-four
  undergraduates were randomized to control, monitoring, reflection, or both
  for five weeks. Monitoring conditions improved self-reported goal attainment;
  SRL scores rose in all groups without between-group differences, and semester
  grades did not significantly differ. The authors note possible unreported
  LLM use and the insensitivity of aggregate grades to a short intervention.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105441).

- **Wagner et al. (2024), feedback plus strategy instruction — Tübingen-hosted author manuscript read.** Three online experiments with university students learning electric-circuit problem solving found brief corrective feedback did not reliably add to prior instruction and could interfere with far transfer; elaborated, actionable feedback combined with instruction produced additive transfer benefits. Reversing order did not change outcomes. Bounded physics task; feedback detail and usefulness matter. [Author manuscript, chapter 4](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/153156/SalomeWagner_Dissertation_Beyond-the-Screen.pdf?isAllowed=n&sequence=2) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101844).

## Generative AI and learning

- **Bastani et al. (2025), high-school mathematics RCT — 68-page author
  manuscript read.** About 1,000 students across roughly 50 classes in one
  Turkish high school took part in four 90-minute sessions. Classroom-level
  assignment compared GPT-4 in a general chat interface, a teacher-informed
  hint-first tutor, and no AI. Both AI arms improved assisted practice scores;
  the general-chat arm scored worse on the immediate unaided exam than control.
  The hint-first tutor largely removed that harm but did not establish an
  unaided-learning gain. Students often copied solutions in the general-chat
  arm and did not recognize the later cost. The study covers one subject, school
  and short-term outcome; it does not test semester retention.
  [Author manuscript](https://hamsabastani.github.io/education_llm.pdf) ·
  [published article](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122).
- **Wang et al. (Tutor CoPilot, 2025 preprint) — full text read.** A
  preregistered RCT assigned about 900 human tutors (serving 1,800 K–12 students
  in underserved U.S. communities) to tutoring with or without real-time AI
  suggestions. The AI supported tutors, who remained responsible for selecting,
  editing or rejecting suggestions. The paper reports four percentage points
  higher topic mastery overall and nine points for students of lower-rated
  tutors, based on session exit tickets. It also reports more guiding questions
  and fewer direct answers in treatment conversations. The result is not a
  delayed independent-transfer measure or a trial of AI replacing tutors.
  [Open full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03017v2).
- **LearnLM Team / Google & Eedi (2025), supervised school RCT — full preprint
  read.** The seven-week exploratory study involved 165 pupils aged 13–15 in
  five UK schools. Human tutors reviewed AI-drafted messages before they reached
  students. The headline next-topic estimate versus human tutoring had a
  credible interval spanning zero; it concerns a same-day item rather than
  seven-week retention. Tutors reported useful Socratic suggestions but edited
  some drafts for pacing and social-emotional nuance. It evaluates supervised
  assistance, not an autonomous tutor.
  [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23633v1).
- **Kestin et al. (2025), university physics RCT — abstract and indexed results
  only.** The publication record describes 194 Harvard students comparing a
  pedagogically designed AI lesson with active-learning instruction. The
  article reports stronger post-lesson learning in less median time for the AI
  group; its indexed text gives a 49-minute AI lesson versus a 60-minute
  classroom period. Publisher access in Chrome stopped at a human-verification
  challenge, which I did not attempt to solve. The abstract does not establish
  semester retention; detailed assessment controls remain unverified.
  [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6) ·
  [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40537565/).
- **Deng et al. (2025), experimental ChatGPT meta-analysis — abstract only.**
  The abstract reports 69 experimental articles from 2022–24, mostly university
  settings and direct student use. It reports positive academic-performance,
  affect/motivation and higher-order-thinking outcomes, lower mental effort,
  and no significant self-efficacy effect. The authors flag limited power
  analyses and concerns about post-intervention assessment design. Full-text
  methods and effect estimates were not extracted here.
  [Publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224).
- **“The impact of GenAI on learning outcomes” (2025), meta-analysis — partial
  publisher-index excerpt only.** The accessible excerpt says 71 studies were
  reviewed and 47 articles (74 effect sizes) entered the overall pooled
  analysis. It reports *g* = .752 overall, with cognitive and affective
  outcomes positive and a behavioral-engagement estimate near zero with a wide
  interval. The full paper, coding decisions and moderators were not read, so
  these are search-indexed figures, not independently checked results.
  [Publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100741).
- **“ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch” (2025), retention RCT — publisher excerpts
  only.** Search-indexed publisher text describes 120 Brazilian business
  undergraduates preparing a presentation with ChatGPT or conventional study,
  with a delayed retention assessment. I did not retrieve the full paper or
  verify the full outcome tables in this pass.
  [Publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssaho.2025.102287).

## Visual explanation and media

- **de Koning et al. (2009), animation cueing framework — full text read.** The
  review organizes visual cue functions as selection (direct attention),
  organization (show structure), and integration (make relationships explicit).
  It is an animation-focused framework/review, not a single controlled trial;
  it notes that cues attracting attention do not guarantee understanding.
  [Full text](https://scispace.com/pdf/towards-a-framework-for-attention-cueing-in-instructional-1knefthuby.pdf).
- **de Koning et al. (2010), cueing in a cardiovascular animation — article
  text read, no OA PDF located.** Forty Dutch psychology undergraduates viewed a
  cardiovascular-system animation with or without spotlight cues. Cues changed
  gaze toward highlighted elements, but did not yield a robust comprehension
  or transfer advantage in this experiment. One animation and a small sample
  limit generalization.
  [Publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475209000103).
- **Ainsworth (2006), DeFT framework — full author-deposited preprint read.**
  The framework asks about representation design, the function of multiple
  representations (complement, constrain, or deepen understanding), and the
  learner tasks required to interpret, select, translate or construct them.
  More representations are not inherently better; proposed heuristics are not
  presented as fixed validated laws.
  [Preprint](https://compassproject.net/Sadhana/teaching/readings/deftainsworth.pdf).
- **Ploetzner et al. (2021), animation versus stills — full text read.** In a
  randomized study of 88 German undergraduates learning a six-bar linkage,
  animation aided immediate motion identification, while a single still image
  aided identification of spatial arrangements. No delayed retention or
  conceptual-transfer test was reported. The result is task-specific evidence
  that display choice can depend on what information is being learned.
  [Full text](https://d-nb.info/1244480126/34) ·
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w).
- **Hegarty (2004), dynamic visualization commentary — full text read.** This
  is a synthesis/commentary rather than a new experiment. It argues against a
  blanket dynamic-over-static rule and asks designers to match display type to
  the target knowledge, domain, learner visualization skill and interaction.
  [Full text](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=892cef14ce420ffd76cbc5db61683451f386100b&repid=rep1&type=pdf).
- **Paas & van Merriënboer (2020), cognitive-load design review — full text
  read.** The short theory review discusses integrated explanations to reduce
  split attention, worked examples for novices, fading as expertise grows, and
  collaboration/gesture as possible supports. It is not a trial of an app or
  visual theme.
  [Full text](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0963721420922183?download=true).
- **Mayer & Fiorella (2014), multimedia design chapter — publisher abstract
  only.** The chapter summary reports experimental support for coherence,
  signaling, redundancy and spatial/temporal contiguity principles, but the
  chapter itself was not accessible in full. It combines prior studies rather
  than presenting one sample or one timing.
  [Publisher chapter record](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139547369.015).
- **Berney & Bétrancourt (2016), animation meta-analysis — full published
  article read from the UNIGE archive.** Fifty papers, 61 studies, 7,036
  participants and 140 animation/static comparisons yielded a small pooled
  advantage for animation (*g* = .226, 95% CI .12–.33), with high heterogeneity
  (I² = 78.38%). Display, domain and outcome varied; the mean effect does not
  justify animating every explanation.
  [Conference paper](https://tecfa.unige.ch/perso/sandra/pdf/Earli2016_berney_betrancourt_FINAL.pdf) ·
  [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2016.06.005).

### Interface structure, navigation, and visual complexity

- **Porta Simó et al. (2015), course map — full text read.** In a three-week online course (n=115), a map bundled outcomes, deadlines, progress and resource links. The interface group completed >4x as many optional tasks; posttest grades did not differ significantly. One-course bundle, no delayed test. [Full text](https://online-journals.org/index.php/i-jet/article/download/4620/3471).
- **Karich et al. (2014), learner-control meta-analysis — full text read.** Across 18 studies/29 effects, adding learner-control options had a near-zero average academic effect (*g* = .05); heterogeneous settings do not support assuming unrestricted choice improves learning. [Author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270114089_Updated_Meta-Analysis_of_Learner_Control_Within_Educational_Technology).
- **Stoesz et al. (2020), LMS visual complexity — full text read.** Two small lab studies (n=29, n=16) rated LMS screenshots. Image metrics predicted perceived complexity, but learning and task completion were not measured; this is usability evidence, not learning efficacy. [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1284134.pdf).
- **Shapiro (2008), hypermedia scaffolding — full text read.** Narrative synthesis: novices may benefit from coherent structure, clear goals and navigation support; exploration can suit learners able to use prior knowledge. This is a framework across older studies, not a validated interface recipe. [Author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226692660_Hypermedia_design_as_learner_scaffolding).

## Neuroscience and memory mechanisms

The earlier [neuroscience note](2026-09-06-neuroscience-learning-evidence.md)
contains ten detailed full-text readings, including retrieval fMRI, spacing and
re-encoding, sleep/relearning, prediction, curiosity, attention and transfer.
Five more accessible studies were read for this update:

- **Wiklund-Hörnqvist et al. (2021), retrieval and hippocampus — full text
  read.** Fifty Swedish upper-secondary students practiced Swahili–Swedish word
  pairs by restudy or retrieval with feedback; delayed fMRI recall followed one
  week later. Correct recall was .53 after retrieval versus .36 after study.
  Successful retrieved items showed greater posterior-hippocampus activity.
  This is an adolescent classroom/associative-memory finding; the neural
  association is not evidence that hippocampal activity measures course mastery.
  [Author full text](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1484125/FULLTEXT02.pdf).
- **Payne et al. (2012), sleep and declarative memory — full PMC text read.**
  Three separate interval cohorts (total *N* = 207) learned related or
  unrelated word pairs. Sleep benefited novel/unrelated associations more than
  semantically related pairs in the 12-hour comparison; time awake was associated
  with more forgetting. Cohorts were not one randomized sleep-by-time factorial,
  and the paired-associate task does not prescribe a study schedule.
  [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3310860/).
- **Landmann et al. (2016), sleep and creative memory — full PMC text read.**
  Sixty university students were assigned to sleep, sleep deprivation or daytime
  wake during a compound-remote-associate task. Sleep strengthened previously
  encoded solutions relative to wake, but did not improve offline
  reorganization; the sleep-deprived group showed more reorganization on some
  problems. This narrow task does not imply sleep generally improves creativity.
  [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4763358/).
- **Schabus et al. (2018), nap/night and declarative/procedural consolidation —
  full-text route read.** The multicentre study compared a 90-minute nap with
  quiet wake for declarative word pairs or procedural mirror tracing and
  related these outcomes to sleep physiology. Nap-versus-wake declarative
  differences were trends; procedural performance worsened during wake and
  improved after a full night. Most sleep features did not predict score change.
  Nap and overnight comparisons were not one factorial randomized experiment.
  [PubMed / PMC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29271015/).
- **Denis et al. (2026), EEG theta and sleep consolidation — full PLOS text
  read.** Thirty-one healthy adults completed two visits, learning word-object
  pairs before either a short nap or quiet wake; item sets were separated across
  immediate and delayed tests to avoid retrieval-practice contamination.
  Associative retention favored nap. Encoding theta related to later sleep
  oscillation/spindle coupling, which related to retention, but theta was not
  itself a direct predictor of retention. Small sample, short nap and
  correlational physiology limit causal or product-level inference.
  [Open paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3003938).
