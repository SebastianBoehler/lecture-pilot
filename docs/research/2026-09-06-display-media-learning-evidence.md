# Learning through displays: evidence for the presentation freeze

Research date: 6 September 2026. Scope: instructional media, diagrams, animation,
interactivity, games and immersive VR. This is a targeted primary-literature review,
not an exhaustive systematic review. Searches used exact titles/authors and topic
queries; claims were checked against publisher articles or academic copies.
Meta-analyses are labeled separately from original experiments. Effect sizes below
describe those studies, not LecturePilot or interchangeable product forecasts.

## Decision

Keep LecturePilot's existing canvas and reviewed component catalogue. Select the
representation from the approved learning operation, then ask the learner to reason
about it. The useful question is what a learner must perceive, explain or change;
the device itself does not establish that learning occurred. The studies below
support specific design hypotheses, with substantial task and comparator limits.

The strongest presentation example is one source-backed comparison or mechanism,
one meaningful prediction, an explanation of the result, then a fresh approved
task. This is an application proposal, not an efficacy finding. Existing focused
attempts and support records remain the evidence boundary.

## Ten inspected sources

### 1. Place explanations next to their referents

**Moreno & Mayer (1999), Journal of Educational Psychology, APA.**
[Original article](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.91.2.358) ·
[Author copy inspected](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Richard-Mayer-4/publication/228698670_Cognitive_Principles_of_Multimedia_Learning_The_Role_of_Modality_and_Contiguity/links/57799c7608aead7ba0764344/Cognitive-Principles-of-Multimedia-Learning-The-Role-of-Modality-and-Contiguity.pdf).

Two experiments with college students inexperienced in meteorology compared
narrated lightning animations with differently placed or timed on-screen text.
Nearby text improved learning relative to separated text; narration also performed
better under tested conditions. Measures included immediate explanation recall,
transfer problems and diagram matching. Scope is a short system-paced science
lesson, not unrestricted reading or a semester course. The paper recruited 132 and
127 students and replaced high-knowledge participants; avoid treating those totals
as analyzed cell counts. This does not justify removing captions, accessibility
text or persistent formulas from a self-paced mathematics interface.

### 2. Let learners advance meaningful segments

**Mayer & Chandler (2001), Journal of Educational Psychology, APA.**
[Original paper inspected](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/Mayer_Chandler01.pdf),
DOI `10.1037/0022-0663.93.2.390`.

Two randomized experiments involved 59 low-meteorology-knowledge university
students, 30 and 29 respectively. Learners saw two presentations of a lightning
animation, either whole or divided into 16 learner-advanced segments. Part-before-
whole and part-before-part sequences improved immediate transfer compared with
their respective controls; retention did not significantly improve. Both groups
received the same explanatory content. This supports testing meaningful pacing,
not a claim that extra clicks always teach better. Small samples, one mechanism,
and immediate outcomes limit generalization. Methods and results were inspected
from scanned pages 394–395, in addition to the abstract.

### 3. Signal causal organization, without adding irrelevant content

**Mautone & Mayer (2001), Journal of Educational Psychology, APA.**
[Original article](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.2.377) ·
[Author-posted abstract inspected](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232494695_Signaling_as_a_Cognitive_Guide_in_Multimedia_Learning).

Three student experiments taught airplane lift through printed text, speech or
narrated animation. Structural signals included an overview, headings and causal
connectives. Signaled lessons produced more solutions on subsequent transfer
questions. The abstract distinguishes signals from new content. This supports
clear subgoals and explicit causal relationships rather than indiscriminate
highlighting. Full methods were not successfully retrieved in this block; do not
quote sample sizes, assignment details or delayed effects from this entry.

### 4. Combine worked steps with principle explanations and fading

**Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill (2003), Journal of Educational Psychology, APA.**
[Original article](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774) ·
[Paper copy inspected](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Making%20the%20most%20of%20examples/Fading%20out%20and%20Prompts.pdf).

Randomized computer-based probability studies used 78 university students and
40 high-school students. Worked examples progressively omitted steps; prompts
asked learners to identify underlying principles. Fading and prompting improved
immediate near/far transfer without significantly increasing study time. The first
experiment found main effects, not a significant interaction: avoid claiming a
special multiplicative synergy. The second compared fading with versus without
prompts. Transfer remained within probability; no months-long or cross-discipline
transfer was demonstrated. The proposal is to use short principle-focused prompts
selectively in teaching, preserving separate approved assessment tasks.

### 5. A diagram plus prediction can outperform passive inspection

**Hegarty, Kriz & Cate (2003), Cognition and Instruction, Lawrence Erlbaum.**
[Original paper inspected](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/hegarty03.pdf),
DOI `10.1207/s1532690xci2104_1`.

Three undergraduate experiments studied a flushing mechanism using static
diagrams, prediction questions, animations and verbal explanations. Samples were
100, 100 and 58; groups were balanced using spatial-ability scores. Prediction
questions and verbal descriptions improved comprehension; animations did not
show superior dynamic understanding over static instructional media. Outcomes
included same-session causal descriptions and troubleshooting. This supports a
prediction-and-explanation design hypothesis, not compulsory prediction before
every concept. A single unfamiliar mechanism and short tests limit transfer to
university mathematics or programming. Equal instructional time was explicitly
controlled in the second experiment, not uniformly across every comparison.

### 6. Use animation when the details of motion are the target

**Ploetzner, Berney & Bétrancourt (2021), Instructional Science, Springer.**
[Full original article inspected](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w).

Eighty-eight university students were randomized to one picture, four pictures
or an animation of a six-bar mechanism. After two-minute exposure, they identified
motions and spatial arrangements; assessment order was counterbalanced. Animation
favored motion identification, while pictures favored spatial arrangements.
These were immediate recognition outcomes, not delayed conceptual transfer.
The animation necessarily conveyed more continuous-motion information, and the
task was a particular mechanical linkage. This qualifies the preceding finding:
static media are not universally superior. Motion timing, acceleration or changing
configuration can justify animation when those features are themselves assessed.

### 7. Presence and a neural-load proxy can diverge from learning

**Makransky, Terkildsen & Mayer (2019), Learning and Instruction, Elsevier.**
[Original article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.12.007) ·
[Full paper copy inspected](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5f8eb6bc5f4fd21c400db907/5fa97946c49e6c72f5f90e38_1-s2.0-S0959475217303274-main.pdf).

Fifty-two university students participated in a counterbalanced mixed experiment:
desktop versus headset science simulations, with text or text plus narration.
Headsets increased reported presence. Desktop knowledge gains were higher in both
lessons (d=.48 and .80); transfer differences were not significant. The EEG-derived
load measure was higher in VR in the second lesson (d=.59). Outcomes followed
the lessons; this is not delayed retention evidence. The transfer measure had low
reliability and the hardware/task were specific. EEG here is an operationalized
proxy, not direct measurement of understanding, synaptic plasticity or individual
mastery. Neither immersion nor a neural signal should replace behavioral evidence.

### 8. VR can help in a particular instructional context

**Makransky & Mayer (2022), Educational Psychology Review, Springer.**
[Full original experiment inspected](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-022-09675-4).

In a six-lesson climate unit, 102 pupils aged 13–16 experienced a Greenland trip
through individual headsets or collective projected video. The headset group had
higher immediate (d=.61) and delayed (d=.70) knowledge scores. Delay averaged
19.44 days, with four intervening lessons and the same knowledge items. Gaze
interaction and individual versus group presentation differed; random assignment
was not established by the inspected text. Therefore this does not isolate
immersion, pure retention of a ten-minute dose, or superiority over interactive
laptops. It is useful counterevidence to a blanket claim that VR harms learning.

### 9. Simulation games depend on their instructional comparator

**Sitzmann (2011), Personnel Psychology, Wiley — meta-analysis.**
[Publisher paper inspected](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2011.01190.x).

The synthesis covered 65 studies and 6,476 trainees. Simulation-game conditions
favored knowledge and retention overall, but the paper identified strong
publication-bias evidence. Benefits depended on active course content, repeated
access and integration with other instruction. Games performed worse when the
comparison instruction actively engaged learners. Populations, tasks, designs
and retention intervals varied; there is no single delayed-effect interval.
The practical inference is to preserve challenge, feedback and opportunities to
practice. It does not establish that points, leaderboards or a game platform are
necessary, nor that an engaging game beats an equally active tutor.

### 10. Gamification has positive but heterogeneous evidence

**Sailer & Homner (2020; online 2019), Educational Psychology Review, Springer — meta-analysis.**
[Full publisher paper inspected](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w).

Cognitive outcomes covered 19 experiments and 1,686 participants, with g=.49
(95% CI .30–.69); motivational and behavioral effects were smaller. Cognitive
effects survived a higher-rigor subset, while motivation/behavior were less stable.
Studies differed in game elements, comparisons, duration and context; this is not
a common delayed retention test. Some extreme effects and duplicate samples were
excluded. Game elements are not equivalent to full instructional games. The result
supports testing a defined design, not importing its pooled effect as a forecast
for LecturePilot badges or treating app activity as learning.

## Fit to current code and the last iteration

Inspected `CanvasInteractiveComponents.tsx`, `CanvasInteractiveChart.tsx`,
`canvas_component_catalog.py` and `course_teaching_instructions.py` in the current
checkout. The app already supports quizzes, interactive charts, process explorers,
data-only visual artifacts and simultaneous mechanism comparisons. Charts already
include data tables and per-frame explanations. The catalogue
already asks for shared scales, units, nearby causal explanations and no arbitrary
executable content. Keep those established boundaries.

| Approved learning operation       | Existing display                    | Proposed refinement                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Compare mechanisms or conditions  | `mechanism_comparison`, grid, table | Keep alternatives simultaneously visible; use the same outcome labels/scales.                                        |
| Trace causal or algorithmic steps | `process_explorer`, flow            | Name each subgoal; ask what the next state should be before revealing it when useful.                                |
| Explain parameter sensitivity     | `interactive_chart`                 | Use the existing prompt text for one directional prediction and reason; then compare with the exact supported frame. |
| Identify a relation or boundary   | Static image, plot, math, paragraph | Show only the relevant representation and place its explanation beside/below it.                                     |
| Learn detailed physical motion    | Existing source video, if justified | Prefer a reviewed source with pause/replay; assess the motion feature, not recall of narration.                      |

These are design inferences from the evidence, not individually validated app
features. A voluntary teaching prediction is not an independent assessment and
must not mark a gate passed. Preserve hidden-bank confidentiality and the current
support-before-reveal rule.

**Small immediate candidate:** refine shared authoring/review guidance to select
media from the observable operation and to use selective prediction or principle
explanation within existing components. Review one real course example through
the published learner flow. Avoid requiring a new prompt, visual or click quota.

**Freeze:** no new VR runtime, points economy, arbitrary simulation execution,
brain-sensing adaptation or renderer family for tomorrow. Future media comparisons
should hold content, practice, feedback and access time as constant as feasible,
then assess a fresh unaided task and later transfer separately from enjoyment.

**Presentation wording:** LecturePilot applies established instructional design
ideas and records separate supported, independent and later evidence. Its own
learning effectiveness remains to be tested. The scientific contribution claimed
today is a defensible design and an inspectable evaluation path.
