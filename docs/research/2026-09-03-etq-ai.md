# ETQ-AI: evidence and implications for LecturePilot

Research note, 2026-09-03. The LinkedIn poster is treated as a pointer, not as
evidence. Claims below rely on the official project page and peer-reviewed work
from the project team.

## Bottom line

ETQ-AI is relevant to LecturePilot, but it solves the opposite side of the
learning loop. Its Teaching Copilot aims to record classroom audio and use audio
plus transcripts to give teachers feedback on instructional organization,
cognitive stimulation, and encouragement and warmth. LecturePilot directly
guides a learner and already records structured, assistance-aware outcomes.

The useful import is therefore a **teaching-process audit**, not classroom
surveillance. LecturePilot can evaluate its own tutor turns against an
educationally grounded rubric because the turns already exist as structured
digital traces. Those process scores should diagnose how the tutor taught; they
must not replace the product's primary authority: delayed, changed-form,
unaided learner performance.

Sources: [official ETQ-AI project page](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/281061),
[peer-reviewed validation study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102264),
and [2024 encouragement-and-warmth study](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-64302-6_5).

## What ETQ-AI is building

The University of Tuebingen describes ETQ-AI as a three-year Hector AI +
Education Future Fund project. Teachers would use a smartphone to record
classroom audio; models would analyze audio and transcripts and return formative
feedback for self-reflection. The named target constructs are:

- instructional organization;
- cognitive stimulation;
- encouragement and warmth.

The page states that the planned system will follow GDPR, the EU AI Act, and
privacy-preserving machine-learning practices. These are project requirements,
not yet evidence that the deployed app satisfies them. Likewise, the poster
award recognizes the presentation; it is not an effectiveness result.

## What the research establishes

The team's 2026 _Learning and Instruction_ article studied 92 recorded German
mathematics lessons from 46 teachers and 1,132 students. Attention-based models
used text, audio, and video features to generate scores for 18 subdimensions of
classroom management, student support, and cognitive activation. The scores
were compared with trained human ratings, reviewed for content plausibility,
and related to student pre/post achievement.

The result is promising but bounded:

- ML scores matched or exceeded human reliability for some subdimensions and
  had lower absolute error than human scores in 11 of 18 subdimensions.
- Experts often judged the generated ratings plausible.
- Predictive validity varied, and the paper states that the ML-generated scores
  did not demonstrate predictive validity for student achievement in this data.
- The study used one German secondary-mathematics setting and confidential
  multimodal data; generalization and actionable real-time feedback remain open.

An earlier AIED 2024 study focused only on encouragement and warmth. It used 367
sixteen-minute segments from the same classroom corpus. The best ensemble of a
multimodal regressor and GPT-4 reached a correlation of `.513` with mean human
ratings, equal to the reported average human inter-rater correlation. GPT-4
alone reached `.341`; GPT-3.5 alone was near zero at `.027`. This supports
machine-assisted rubric coding, not autonomous judgment of teaching quality.

## Relationship to LecturePilot

| Layer                   | Question                                                       | Appropriate signal                                                                           | Authority         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Tutor process           | Did the tutor use sound instructional practice?                | Rubric-coded tutor trace: organization, cognitive activation, calibrated scaffolding, warmth | Diagnostic        |
| Immediate learning loop | Did the learner recover after bounded help?                    | Assistance-aware supported retry                                                             | Secondary outcome |
| Durable learning        | Can the learner later perform independently on a changed task? | Revision-bound delayed transfer                                                              | Primary outcome   |

ETQ-AI contributes the first layer. StudentSim contributes an offline hypothesis
about how a learner may respond to guidance. LecturePilot's real learner loop
must retain authority over the latter two layers. A polished, warm, Socratic
tutor message is not evidence that learning occurred.

## Smallest useful adoption

Define a private, offline audit rubric for sampled tutor turns and canvas plans:

1. **Organization:** Was the goal and next step clear?
2. **Cognitive activation:** Did the learner have to retrieve, explain, compare,
   or decide instead of receiving the answer?
3. **Adaptive support:** Did help respond to the observed attempt and stay within
   the approved scaffold level?
4. **Fading:** Did the next check reduce help and require independent evidence?
5. **Supportive climate:** Was feedback respectful, specific, and encouraging
   without empty praise?
6. **Grounding:** Was the instruction faithful to unlocked course evidence?

Report each dimension separately and retain the exact trace for human review.
Then test whether the rubric scores predict independent exit performance and
delayed transfer. If they do not, the rubric remains a process-quality aid and
must not be optimized as a learning reward.

## Do not import

- Do not add ambient classroom audio or video collection to LecturePilot. It
  creates a substantially harder consent, bystander, retention, bias, and legal
  problem than auditing the tutor traces the system already produces.
- Do not reduce a high-inference construct such as cognitive activation to
  speech rate, sentiment, question count, or another easy behavioral proxy.
- Do not expose raw learner conversations in professor analytics. Preserve the
  existing categorical, revision-bound outcome boundary.
- Do not let an LLM grade its own teaching without calibrated human-coded data,
  subgroup checks, uncertainty reporting, and validation against learner
  outcomes.

## Strategic research fit

The strongest collaboration proposition is not “we also have an AI tutor.” It
is: **ETQ-AI supplies validated teaching-quality constructs; LecturePilot
supplies controlled tutor traces plus assistance-aware learner outcomes.** A
joint study could ask whether process scores for cognitive activation,
scaffolding, and supportive feedback predict immediate independent performance
and delayed transfer. That would test the missing link between looking like
good teaching and producing durable learning.
